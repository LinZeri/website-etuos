Your Google Business Profile is the single biggest factor in whether your business shows up on Google Maps. Not your website. Not your backlinks. Your profile.

In 2026, Whitespark's annual survey of 47 expert local SEOs found that Google Business Profile signals account for 32% of what determines local pack ranking ([Whitespark, "2026 Local Search Ranking Factors", 2026](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/), accessed Sep. 2026). That makes your profile the largest ranking lever you control. And most service business owners claimed theirs years ago, filled out half the fields, and never went back.

An incomplete profile is worse than no profile. It tells Google you exist but gives no reason to rank you above the competitor down the street who added photos last Tuesday. This checklist covers every section of your Google Business Profile in priority order, so you fix the highest-impact items first and stop guessing what matters.

## Should You Verify Your Profile Before Anything Else?

Yes. An unverified profile can appear in limited contexts, but it will not rank consistently in the local pack. Google treats verification as proof that you are the legitimate business owner, and until you complete it, you lose access to the tools that actually drive calls.

Google requires verification before a business profile can rank consistently in local results. The fastest method in most markets is video verification, where you record a short clip showing your location, signage, or work vehicle. Some businesses still receive the postcard option, which takes five to ten business days. Once verified, you unlock Google Posts, review management, Insights data, and messaging.

If you haven't verified yet, nothing else on this checklist matters. Start there. Everything else builds on a verified foundation.

## How Do You Choose the Right Primary Category?

Your primary category is the strongest relevance signal Google reads from your profile. In 2026, Whitespark's expert panel ranked primary category as the number-one individual factor within the GBP signals group ([Whitespark, 2026](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/), accessed Sep. 2026). Getting this wrong costs you visibility on every search.

Choosing the most specific primary category on your Google Business Profile is the single fastest way to improve relevance. A general contractor who primarily does HVAC work should list "HVAC Contractor," not "General Contractor." Google matches your primary category against the searcher's query before considering any other profile signal.

Here's how to find the right one. Search your service plus your city on Google Maps and look at the top three listings. What category do they use? That's usually the most relevant match. Google offers over 4,000 categories, and narrow categories outperform broad ones almost every time.

A wrong primary category keeps a profile out of the searches it should win. An HVAC company listed as "General Contractor" is competing in the wrong pool of searches, and switching the primary category to "HVAC Contractor" puts the profile in front of the searches that match the work.

Secondary categories help too. A house cleaning company might set "House Cleaning Service" as primary and add "Carpet Cleaning Service" and "Move-In Cleaning Service" as secondary. But the primary category carries the weight.

## What Does a Complete Profile Look Like?

In 2026, 83% of consumers use Google as their primary source for reading reviews about local businesses ([BrightLocal, "Local Consumer Review Survey 2025", 2025, via SearchLab Digital](https://searchlabdigital.com/blog/data-from-brightlocals-consumer-review-survey/), accessed Sep. 2026). When those consumers find your listing, every empty field is a missed opportunity. A complete Google Business Profile gives Google more signals to match your business to relevant local searches, and gives customers fewer reasons to look elsewhere.

Here is every field that matters, in order:

- **Business name.** Use your exact legal name. Do not add keywords or city names. Google penalizes keyword stuffing in business names.
- **Address vs. service area.** If customers come to you (a storefront), show your address. If you go to them (a plumber, a cleaner, an electrician), hide the address and set your service area by city or zip code. This is called a service-area business (SAB) setup.
- **Phone number.** Use a local number, not a toll-free one. Local numbers build trust and match the area you serve.
- **Website URL.** Add UTM parameters so you can track clicks from your profile separately in Google Analytics. Something like `?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp`.
- **Hours.** Set your regular hours. Add holiday hours and special hours when they apply. Being open at the time someone searches measurably helps your ranking, so keep this accurate.
- **Business description.** You get 750 characters. Lead with your primary service and city. Write for the customer, not for Google, but include natural keywords.
- **Attributes.** Mark what applies: woman-owned, veteran-owned, wheelchair accessible, online appointments. These show up in your listing and help customers filter results.

## Why Should You Add Photos Every Week?

BrightLocal analyzed over 45,000 Google Business Profile listings (580,853 images total) and found that listings with 100+ images receive 520% more calls and 2,717% more direction requests than the average listing ([BrightLocal, "GBP Insights Study"](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/google-my-business-insights-study/), accessed Sep. 2026). Photo volume correlates directly with the actions that put money in your pocket.

For a service business, this means photographing completed jobs, your team at work, your vehicle, and your workspace regularly. One real photo per week builds a library that outperforms competitors relying on a handful of stock images. Here's what to shoot:

- **Exterior shots.** Your storefront, office, or branded vehicle. This helps customers recognize you and confirms your location.
- **Team photos.** People trust people. Show your crew doing actual work, not posed stock-photo smiles.
- **Completed jobs.** Before-and-after photos are powerful for contractors, cleaners, and landscapers.
- **Interior.** Your office, showroom, or workspace. Clean, well-lit, honest.

Skip stock photos entirely. Google's image recognition can detect them, and customers see through them immediately. A grainy photo of your actual team finishing a real job outperforms a polished stock image every time.

Upload at least one photo per week. You don't need a photographer. A phone in good light is enough. The compounding effect over a year is enormous: 52 photos turns into real visibility.

## How Do You Build a Review Engine That Drives Ranking?

Review signals account for 20% of local pack ranking, according to the same Whitespark 2026 survey ([Whitespark, 2026](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/), accessed Sep. 2026). And consumer expectations around star ratings are rising fast. In 2026, 31% of consumers will only use a business rated 4.5 stars or higher, nearly double the 17% from 2025 ([BrightLocal, "Local Consumer Review Survey 2026", 2026](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/), accessed Sep. 2026).

Reviews drive 20% of local pack ranking and directly affect whether a customer calls you. In 2026, 31% of consumers will only use a business rated 4.5 stars or higher, nearly double the prior year. Responding to every review, positive or negative, signals to Google that the business is active and engaged.

The review strategy that works for service businesses is simple:

1. **Ask in person, right after the job.** Satisfaction is highest the moment you finish. Hand the customer your phone with the review link open, or text them the link within the hour.
2. **Make it one tap.** Use Google's review link shortcut (search "Google review link generator" for your business). Drop it into a text message. Don't make them hunt for your listing.
3. **Respond to every review.** Positive reviews get a short, personal thank you. Negative reviews get a calm, professional response that addresses the issue. In 2026, 80% of consumers say they're more likely to use a business that replies to all reviews (BrightLocal, 2026).
4. **Never offer incentives.** Google's policies prohibit exchanging anything of value for reviews. If caught, you lose reviews and can lose your listing.

Also, 74% of consumers check at least two review sites before choosing a local business ([BrightLocal, "Local Consumer Review Survey 2025", 2025, via SearchLab Digital](https://searchlabdigital.com/blog/data-from-brightlocals-consumer-review-survey/), accessed Sep. 2026). Your Google reviews matter most, but don't ignore Yelp, Facebook, or industry-specific platforms.

Recency matters more than total count. A business with a steady flow of reviews from the last few months can outrank a competitor with far more reviews that stopped coming in a year ago. In 2026, 74% of consumers only care about reviews written in the last three months (BrightLocal, 2026).

## Are You Using Google Posts, Q&A, and Products?

In 2026, 47% of US adults reported using an AI tool for local search in the previous month, according to a Yext survey of 1,120 adults ([Yext, "2026 Consumer Search Behaviors Report", 2026](https://www.yext.com/resources/consumer-search-behaviors-findings), accessed Sep. 2026; Yext sells local presence management software). Google Posts, Q&A answers, and the Products section give your profile additional content that Google can index and AI search tools can cite. Most competitors leave these sections empty.

Google Posts, Q&A, and the Products/Services sections are free engagement tools most competitors ignore. Posting weekly keeps your profile active in Google's eyes and gives AI search surfaces more content to cite. Here's how to use each one:

**Google Posts.** Publish a short update every week. Include a photo and a call to action (call now, book online, learn more). Keep posts under 300 words. Topics can be seasonal tips, completed projects, special offers, or team news.

**Q&A.** Don't wait for customers to ask. Seed this section with the questions your customers actually ask on the phone. "Do you offer free estimates?" "What areas do you serve?" "Are you licensed and insured?" Write clear, direct answers. Monitor for spam questions and report them.

**Products and Services.** List every service you offer with a short description and a price range if applicable. This is especially valuable for [service businesses across the US](/en/usa) because it gives Google explicit signals about what you do, beyond your category alone.

## Can You Reach Customers Who Search in More Than One Language?

Every other guide to Google Business Profile optimization treats the profile as monolingual. In practice, a cleaning company in [Miami](/en/cities/miami) or a contractor in Newark with an English-only profile is invisible to customers who search in Spanish or Portuguese. Adding a multilingual description captures searches competitors leave on the table.

In markets like Miami, Houston, or Newark, a significant share of your potential customers search in Spanish or Portuguese. An English-only profile misses them entirely. Here is what you can do:

- **Add a Spanish-language business description.** Google supports profiles in multiple languages. Write a natural business description in Spanish (or Portuguese) that mirrors the intent of your English description, not a word-for-word translation.
- **Match your website content.** If your profile links to a page in English but the customer searched in Spanish, you'll lose them. Consider building a [bilingual or multilingual website](/en/blog/selling-to-english-spanish-portuguese-speaking-customers) that matches what your customers expect.
- **Check your category availability.** Some Google categories appear differently in other languages. Make sure your primary category resolves correctly for Spanish or Portuguese searches in your area.

This is a structural gap most competitors never address. In cities with large bilingual populations, it's one of the fastest ways to pick up calls your competitor's profile can't capture.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Claiming, verifying, and optimizing your Google Business Profile costs nothing. Google charges only if you choose to run ads through Google Ads. The profile itself, including Posts, Q&A, reviews, and Insights, is free for every business.

### How long does it take for changes to show up?

Most edits appear within 24 to 48 hours. Verification by video takes a few business days; verification by postcard takes five to ten business days. Some changes (like a new primary category) may take up to a week for Google to process and reflect in local rankings.

### Does my Google Business Profile affect my website's organic ranking?

Indirectly. GBP signals drive local pack rankings, which operate as a separate system from organic search results. A strong profile builds brand prominence online, which can help branded organic queries over time. But the direct impact is on the map pack, not on your website's position in the ten blue links.

### How do I get more reviews without being pushy?

Ask in person, right after a completed job, when satisfaction is highest. Send a follow-up text with a direct review link within the hour. The key is timing and ease: one tap, no hunting for your listing. Never offer discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews. Google's policies prohibit it, and customers sense it.

### Can I rank in the map pack for cities where I don't have an office?

Yes, if you are a service-area business. Define your service area in your GBP settings and build a [unique landing page for each city](/en/services/seo) on your website. Proximity still matters: ranking in distant cities requires stronger prominence signals (reviews, links, authority) to overcome the distance disadvantage.

## What Should You Fix First?

Your Google Business Profile is not a set-it-and-forget-it asset. Verify it, pick the most specific primary category, complete every field, add photos every week, build reviews systematically, post and use Q&A, and address the multilingual gap if your market calls for it.

The order matters. Verification first. Category second. Then completeness, photos, reviews, and ongoing activity. Each item on this checklist compounds the one before it.
