A customer in Houston types "taller de frenos cerca de mí" into Google. Your shop does brakes, and it is two miles away. But your ads are written in English, so you never enter the conversation. Or worse, you do, with a Spanish headline that was pushed through a translation tool and sounds like a legal notice.

Both cases cost you money. This guide explains how Spanish language Google Ads work for a local business, what just changed in how Google reads language, and how to build a campaign that a Spanish-speaking customer actually trusts.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - A Spanish campaign starts with Spanish searches, not with your English ad. Build the keyword list, the ad copy and the landing page in Spanish, together.
> - Google is changing how it handles language in Search campaigns: it will rely on the language of the creative and the landing page ([Search Engine Journal, 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-is-removing-language-targeting-from-search-campaigns/585592/), accessed Oct. 2026).
> - Keep Spanish in its own campaign, so you can see its leads, its search terms and its budget on their own.
> - Never write "hablamos español" unless a person who speaks Spanish answers the phone.

## What Changed in Google Ads Language Targeting?

![A large gray switch with a green lever beside two simple page cards, one green and one light gray](/images/blog/spanish-language-google-ads-for-local-business-1-language-signal.webp)

For years, a Google Ads campaign had a language setting, and many advertisers treated it as the main way to reach Spanish speakers. That is changing.

According to Search Engine Journal, Google Ads is changing language targeting in September 2026. Starting in late September, the campaign-level language setting is removed for Search campaigns, and "Google will rely on the language of the creative and landing page." In Performance Max, the change applies to Search, and the campaign-level language setting is still used elsewhere ([Search Engine Journal, 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-is-removing-language-targeting-from-search-campaigns/585592/), accessed Oct. 2026). The same article advises advertisers to make sure ads and landing pages use the intended languages.

Read that plainly. For Search, the language of your ad and your landing page is now the signal Google uses. A box in the campaign settings is no longer what tells Google "this one is for Spanish speakers." What you write is.

That raises the stakes on the work most local businesses skip. If your Spanish ad sends people to an English page, or your English ad has one stray Spanish phrase, you are giving Google and the customer mixed signals. Keep each ad, each page and each call to action in one language, on purpose.

## Why Does a Translated Ad Not Work as a Spanish Campaign?

A translated ad takes your English headline, runs it through a tool and publishes it. A Spanish campaign starts from the customer: what they type, what worries them and what makes them trust a stranger with their car, their home or their health.

Start with who you are talking to. In the Census Bureau's 2018 to 2022 American Community Survey, 78.3% of people age 5 and older spoke only English at home, and among those who spoke another language, 61.1% spoke Spanish ([U.S. Census Bureau, 2023](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023/language-at-home-acs-5-year.html), accessed Oct. 2026). The same release says 61.0% of Spanish speakers spoke English "very well." Notice what that does not say. Speaking English well is not the same as preferring it. Someone who can handle a form in English may still search for a mechanic in Spanish, because that is the language of their home and of the people who recommend businesses to them.

Searches are where the gap shows up first. "Auto repair near me" and "taller mecánico cerca de mí" are two different queries, not one query in two languages. The words differ, the local phrases differ, and often the competitors differ. Spanish also varies by country of origin. The word for a part, a tool or a type of shop can change between Mexico, Colombia, Puerto Rico and Cuba, so a single translated keyword list will miss phrases your customers really type.

There is a second problem: the reader. The translation tool does not know that "Get a free quote today" sounds like a sales pitch in Spanish, or that a customer who is worried about being misunderstood needs to hear that someone will explain the work in plain words. Copy has to be written, not converted.

## Should You Run Separate Spanish Campaigns or One Campaign?

![Two separate folders, one gray and one green, each holding its own page and a stack of coins](/images/blog/spanish-language-google-ads-for-local-business-2-separate-campaigns.webp)

For most local businesses, run Spanish as its own campaign. Not a copy of the English one with a language box changed, but a campaign with its own keywords, its own ads, its own landing page and its own budget line. Here is why that structure holds up better.

- **You can read the results.** When Spanish and English share a campaign, the numbers blend. You cannot tell whether a lead came cheaply from Spanish or expensively from English. Separate campaigns give you separate reports.
- **The keywords are different.** A Spanish keyword list is its own list, and it needs its own negative keywords too. English negatives do not block Spanish queries you do not want.
- **The budget is yours to move.** If Spanish is bringing good calls, you can give it more. If it is not working yet, you can pause it without touching the English side.
- **The language signal stays clean.** Since Google now looks at the language of the creative and landing page, a campaign where every asset is Spanish sends one clear signal.

One point to keep in mind about the auction. Google says your maximum bid is a ceiling, and that what you actually pay is often less. The Ad Rank also depends on the quality of your ads and landing page, the competitiveness of the auction and the context of the person's search ([Google, "About Ad Rank", Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722122), accessed Oct. 2026). In plain terms, a Spanish ad that matches what the person searched for, and sends them to a page that matches the ad, is working with the system instead of against it.

## How Do You Write Spanish Ad Copy That Sounds Native?

Write for the customer first and the character count second. A few rules help.

**1. Start from the Spanish search, not the English ad.** Write down how a customer would describe the problem out loud: "se me prendió la luz del motor," "no me enfría el aire," "tengo una gotera en el techo." Then build the headline around those words.

**2. Pick one voice and keep it.** Spanish gives you a choice between "tú" and "usted." Many local service ads do well with a respectful "usted" for older customers and a friendlier "tú" for younger ones. Choose based on who your customer is, and do not switch halfway through an ad.

**3. Answer the worry before the click.** A customer may be asking: "Will they understand me? Will the price change later?" A line that says an estimate is explained in plain words and the price is agreed before work starts speaks to that. A translated "Best prices in town" does not.

**4. Use local words, not textbook words.** Have a native speaker from your own community read the draft. If your customers are mostly Mexican, Colombian or Puerto Rican, the right word may differ. A short read-through from a real customer or employee catches things no tool will.

**5. Do not promise what you cannot staff.** The line "Hablamos español" is a promise. If the first call goes to a voicemail in English, that promise breaks, and the click you paid for is lost.

Here is an illustration of the difference, using an auto repair shop. These are examples of the approach, not tested ads:

| Translated approach | Written for the customer |
|---|---|
| "Reparación de automóviles profesional. Llame hoy." | "Revisión de frenos en Houston. Te explicamos el trabajo antes de empezar." |
| "Obtenga una cotización gratis." | "Pide tu presupuesto. Te lo damos claro, sin sorpresas." |
| "Los mejores precios." | "Atención en español, de principio a fin." |

The right column only works if the shop can really deliver it. That is the test for every line you write.

## What Does the Landing Page Need?

![A laptop showing a simple page layout with a green button, beside a desk phone with green sound waves](/images/blog/spanish-language-google-ads-for-local-business-3-landing-page.webp)

The click is only the first step. What happens after it decides whether the ad paid off.

Google's own Quality Score documentation lists landing page experience as one of the three components behind the score, along with expected click-through rate and ad relevance. It also says Quality Score is not an input in the ad auction, but a diagnostic tool ([Google, "About Quality Score for Search campaigns", Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118), accessed Oct. 2026). Use it as a mirror: if the page is weak, it tells you where to look.

For a Spanish campaign, the landing page should meet these basics:

- **The page is in Spanish**, from the headline to the form and the button. Not a translated paragraph on top of an English page.
- **The offer matches the ad.** If the ad says "presupuesto claro para frenos," the page opens with brakes and a clear estimate, not a general homepage.
- **There is a way to reach a person in Spanish.** A phone number that answers in Spanish, or a WhatsApp number that someone checks in Spanish. WhatsApp is a common way to reach a business, so it is worth offering if you will really answer it.
- **The form is short.** Name, phone and what they need. Every extra field is a reason to leave.
- **The details are consistent.** Hours, service area and name should match your Google Business Profile. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/en/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers that side.

Language also affects trust in general. A CSA Research survey of 8,709 consumers in 29 countries found that 76% of online shoppers prefer to buy products with information in their native language ([CSA Research, 2020](https://csa-research.com/l/media/Consumers-Prefer-their-Own-Language), accessed Oct. 2026). That figure is global and about product information, not about Spanish speakers in the US or about search. Still, it supports a simple point: a page in the customer's language removes one more reason to hesitate.

## How Do You Know If the Campaign Is Working?

![A magnifying glass over a list of blank lines, with one line highlighted in green](/images/blog/spanish-language-google-ads-for-local-business-4-search-terms.webp)

Give the campaign a fair look, without expecting a fixed timeline. Results depend on competition, your area and how well the pieces match.

Start with research before launch. Google's Keyword Planner helps you research keywords for Search campaigns and shows estimates of volume and average cost, though you must complete your account setup with billing information to access basic features ([Google, "Use Keyword Planner", Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243), accessed Oct. 2026). Use it as a starting point and not as a promise. Google's own page on top-of-page bid estimates says an estimate is not a guarantee ([Google, "Top of page bid estimate: Definition", Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6292661), accessed Oct. 2026).

After launch, check these things regularly:

1. **The search terms report.** This shows what people actually typed. Add the good phrases as keywords and block the wrong ones. This is where a native Spanish list gets sharper over time.
2. **Cost per lead, in Spanish and in English, separately.** Average cost per click is simply total cost divided by total clicks ([Google, "Average cost-per-click (Avg. CPC): Definition", Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14074), accessed Oct. 2026). It tells you what a click cost, not whether the click became a customer. Look at calls and booked jobs, too.
3. **The quality of the calls.** Listen to a few. Did the person who answered understand the question? Did the caller get a clear next step?
4. **Where the lead drops off.** If clicks are fine and calls are weak, look at the page and the phone, not the ad.

Be patient with changes. Adjust one thing at a time, so you know what moved the result.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Google Ads still let me choose Spanish as a campaign language?

Not for Search campaigns after late September 2026. According to Search Engine Journal, the campaign-level language setting is removed for Search, and Google relies on the language of the creative and landing page. In Performance Max, the change applies to Search, and the campaign-level language setting is still used elsewhere.

### Is it enough to translate my English ads into Spanish?

No. Translated copy keeps the English structure and misses the words people actually search. Write Spanish ads from Spanish searches, build a separate keyword list, and have a native speaker from your community review the text.

### Should I run Spanish ads in a separate campaign?

For most local businesses, yes. A separate campaign keeps keywords, negative keywords, budget and results apart, so you can see what Spanish is doing without it blending into English numbers.

### What language should my landing page be in?

The same language as the ad. Google now relies on the language of the creative and landing page, and the customer expects the page to continue the conversation the ad started.

### Do I need someone who speaks Spanish before I start?

Yes, at least for the first conversation. A Spanish ad that leads to an English-only phone line wastes the click. Decide who answers calls, texts and WhatsApp messages in Spanish before you spend on ads.

### How much will Spanish language Google Ads cost?

It depends on your service, your city and the competition, and there is no fixed number that applies to every business. Use Keyword Planner to see estimates for your own market, and remember that Google says a bid estimate is not a guarantee.

## What Should You Do Next?

Before you spend anything, do three things. Check which of your current calls, messages and reviews already arrive in Spanish. Decide who will answer in Spanish. Then build one small Spanish campaign with its own keywords, ads and landing page, and read its results separately.
