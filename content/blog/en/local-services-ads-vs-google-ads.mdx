Local Services Ads and Google Search Ads both put your business in front of someone who is ready to hire. They charge for different things. Local Services Ads charge you for a valid lead. Google Search Ads charge you for a click. That one difference changes how you budget, how you measure, and which trades should start where.

Google's help pages describe the pay-per-lead model plainly: "You still only pay for valid leads (such as phone calls and messages) rather than ad clicks" ([Google, "Local Services Ads transition to Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals", Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585), accessed Sep. 2026). Search Ads work the other way: if someone clicks your ad, you pay for the click, up to the maximum cost-per-click bid you set ([Google, "About maximum CPC bidding", Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6326), accessed Sep. 2026).

This guide compares the two for a local service business. It covers who qualifies, what counts as a lead, what is changing this year, and how to choose. It does not quote average prices, because the price of a lead depends on your trade, your city, and the week you ask. For the cost side of paid ads, read our [guide to what paid ads cost a local business](/en/blog/how-much-paid-ads-cost-local-business-guide).

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Local Services Ads charge per valid lead (a call, a message, a booking). Google Search Ads charge per click.
> - Not every trade or city qualifies for Local Services Ads. Google screens each business, and the checks vary by category and region.
> - Local Services Ads are moving into Google Ads campaigns with pay-per-lead goals, in phases that began in August 2026 for select US home and storefront service advertisers.
> - If you do not answer calls and messages, your Local Services Ads ranking can suffer. The ad only pays off if someone picks up.

## What Is the Real Difference Between Local Services Ads and Google Ads?

Local Services Ads bill you for a valid lead, such as a phone call or a message. Google Search Ads bill you for a click on your ad, whether or not the person calls. Everything else in the comparison follows from that: how you budget, what you measure, and how much control you have.

**Local Services Ads** let you "advertise your business on Google and receive leads directly from potential customers," and those leads arrive as "phone calls and messages sent through your Local Services ad" ([Google, "Getting started with Local Services Ads", Local Services Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841), accessed Sep. 2026). The ads show up prominently in Google Search results when people search for the services you offer in the areas you chose.

**Google Search Ads** are the classic text ads you build keyword by keyword. You choose the keywords, write the ads, pick the landing page, and set bids. You have more control, and you also carry more of the work.

Think of it this way. Local Services Ads are a lead product with a screening step. Search Ads are a traffic product where you build the whole funnel.

## Are Local Services Ads Changing in 2026?

Yes. Google is moving Local Services Ads into Google Ads, in phases. According to Google, "August 2026: The first phase of the migration begins for select home and storefront service advertisers in the United States," and "2027: Non-U.S. accounts, and all remaining business categories" complete the transition ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585), accessed Sep. 2026).

Here is what Google says changes after your account is migrated:

- You manage the campaign inside Google Ads. Google states that "you will no longer be able to access your original Local Services Ads dashboard."
- Manual bidding goes away. Your historical average weekly budget is divided by 7 to become a daily average budget.
- Historical reports do not transfer, so Google advises saving your performance data before you migrate.

Here is what stays the same: you still pay only for valid leads, and your ads continue to show exclusively on Google Search and Google Maps in the same positions as before, and lead history transfers automatically.

One naming note. Older articles, including plenty of agency blogs, call the trust badge "Google Guaranteed." Google's current Local Services help page calls it the Google Verified badge. If you are searching for the program under the old name, you will find outdated advice. Use the new name when you read Google's help pages.

## What Counts as a Lead, and What Do You Actually Pay For?

You pay for valid leads only. A valid lead is a real customer contact, such as a phone call you answer, a message, a voicemail, or a booking request. Google assesses leads when the customer first reaches out, and low-quality leads can be credited.

Google's help page lists the contacts that count ([Google, "How leads work", Local Services Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435), accessed Sep. 2026):

- **Messages:** a text message or email from the customer.
- **Voicemails:** a voicemail from the customer, or a customer who meaningfully engages with an automated system.
- **Phone calls:** a call you answer and speak with the customer.
- **Missed calls with follow-up:** a missed call without a voicemail, where you return the customer's message.
- **Bookings:** a booking request from a customer (United States and Canada only).

The same page says message leads are typically priced lower than the corresponding phone lead, and that factors such as the service requested and the customer's engagement history affect pricing.

Some contacts are not charged. Google's examples include contacts received outside business hours, customers asking for advice rather than booking a service, research-only inquiries, customers who do not respond to your follow-up, and service mismatches with what you advertise. If you think a charged lead was low quality, you can say so in the Feedback Survey for that lead, and Google may automatically credit leads it later judges to be a problem.

This is the biggest practical advantage for a small owner. You do not pay for a click from someone who was never going to call. But you still pay for a real call that does not turn into a job, so your close rate matters as much as it does with any other channel.

## Who Qualifies for Local Services Ads?

Only businesses that pass Google's screening. Local Services Ads are not open to every trade in every city, and that is the first thing to check before you plan a budget around them.

Google says that businesses advertising directly "undergo screening procedures that vary by category and region but may include license, insurance, and background checks" ([Google, "How providers qualify for Local Services Ads", Local Services Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6230381), accessed Sep. 2026). The checks can include:

- A business entity check.
- A business owner background check.
- A service professional background check.

Google also verifies that advertisers hold the applicable state, provincial, or country-level licenses. Whenever applicable, each business must be insured, and the insurance type and minimum amount depend on the category and location.

Passing that process is what earns the badge. Google describes it as signaling "to consumers that your business has passed Google's proprietary screening process" (Local Services Help, accessed Sep. 2026).

What this means for you: if you are a licensed, insured contractor who can pass background checks, Local Services Ads are worth a serious look. If you are a newer company still sorting out licensing or insurance, that paperwork is your first job, not the ad account. Search Ads do not have this screening step, so they are the faster way to start when Local Services Ads are not yet available to you.

## Which Trades Should Start Where?

Start with Local Services Ads if your trade and city qualify and your business depends on phone calls from people who need a job done soon. Start with Search Ads if your category is not eligible, if you sell something people research before they call, or if you want control over the exact searches you show up for.

Use these questions instead of a one-size rule:

1. **Is your category eligible in your city?** Check Google's sign-up flow for your trade. Eligibility changes by category and region.
2. **How fast do people decide?** Emergency and same-week jobs, like a burst pipe or a broken AC, fit a pay-per-lead ad that shows near the top of the results. Considered purchases, like a full remodel, often need more room to explain your work, which Search Ads and a good landing page give you.
3. **Can you answer every call?** Google warns that "if you regularly fail to answer calls or respond to messages, your ad ranking may be affected" (Local Services Help, accessed Sep. 2026). A business that cannot pick up is a poor fit for a lead product.
4. **Do you have the licenses and insurance?** If not, plan for Search Ads first.
5. **Do you need to control keywords and messaging?** If you want to bid on specific services or write your own ad copy, that is Search Ads territory.

## When Should You Run Both?

Run both when you have the capacity to answer every lead and you want to cover two different moments in the customer's search. Local Services Ads catch people who want to hire a verified local pro now. Search Ads catch people searching for a specific service, a specific problem, or a service in a specific neighborhood that you want to own.

Running both also gives you a comparison you cannot get from theory. Track each channel separately, and judge them by the same three numbers: how many leads, how many became booked jobs, and what each booked job cost you. Do not judge by clicks or by the number of calls alone. A cheap lead that never books is not cheap.

Two rules keep this clean:

- **Give each channel its own tracking.** Use separate call tracking or lead tags, so you know which channel produced the job.
- **Write down your close rate before you start.** If you close a fraction of your leads, your real cost per job is your cost per lead divided by that fraction. Know that number before you decide a channel is expensive.

## Do Local Services Ads Replace Your Google Business Profile?

No. They work alongside it. Your profile is what people find when they look you up after seeing your ad, and it is a free channel that builds trust while ads are running. A profile with no reviews, wrong hours, or no photos undermines every ad you buy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are Local Services Ads worth it for a small local business?

They can be, if you qualify, you answer your phone, and you close the leads you get. The model protects you from paying for clicks that never call. It does not protect you from leads that do not turn into jobs. Measure booked jobs and cost per booked job, not lead count.

### Do I pay for bad leads?

Google assesses each lead when the customer first reaches out and does not charge for leads it finds invalid or low quality. If you believe a charged lead was low quality, use the Feedback Survey for that lead. Google may also issue credits automatically when its models later judge a lead to be low quality ([Google, "How leads work"](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435), accessed Sep. 2026).

### What happened to the Google Guaranteed badge?

Google's current Local Services help pages call the trust badge the Google Verified badge, and they describe it as the sign that a business passed Google's screening. If you see "Google Guaranteed" on older articles, treat the rest of the advice with care and check it against Google's current help pages.

### How do I set a budget for Local Services Ads?

You set an average weekly budget based on the number of leads you want in a typical week. After an account moves into Google Ads, Google divides that historical weekly budget by 7 to set a daily average budget ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585), accessed Sep. 2026). Start from the number of jobs you can actually take on, not from a dollar figure you saw online.

### Can I run Local Services Ads and Google Search Ads at the same time?

Many local service businesses use both, because they capture different searches. Track each one separately so you can see which produces booked jobs at a cost you can afford.

### Do I need a website for these ads?

Search Ads send people to a landing page, so yes. For Local Services Ads, your Google Business Profile and your reviews do a lot of the trust work, but a clear website still helps people who look you up before they call. Build the foundation first, then add spend.

## What Should You Do First?

Check whether your trade and city qualify for Local Services Ads, gather your license and insurance paperwork, and make sure someone can answer every call. Then decide whether to start with Local Services Ads, Search Ads, or both, using your own close rate as the guide.
