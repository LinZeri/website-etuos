You set up your Google Business Profile. Your hours are right, your photos are up, and a few customers have left reviews. Then someone asks about your website, and you wonder if you even need one. The profile already shows your phone number and a map pin, after all.

Short answer: yes, a business that wants to grow on its own terms should have a website. The profile is a good first step, and it does a job a website cannot do. But the two are different tools, and one does not stand in for the other. This post explains what each one is for, where the profile runs out of room and how to think about the pair. It does not walk through profile setup or website fixes. Those have their own guides, linked below.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - A Google Business Profile and a website do different jobs. The profile helps people find and contact your business on Google. The website is the place you fully control.
> - Google says local ranking is based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that prominence also reflects how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have.
> - The profile has rules and limits on what you can show. A website is where you can explain everything else.
> - You do not need a big site. A simple, clear one is a real asset.

## Can a Google Business Profile Replace a Website?

No. A profile is a listing that Google hosts and Google's rules shape. A website is a property you own and design. They overlap a little, since both show who you are and how to reach you, but they are built for different moments.

The profile is built for the moment someone is searching and deciding. The website is built for the moment someone wants to know more, or wants to see proof that you are the right business. If you only have one, you are missing half of that picture.

Also remember that the profile itself points to a website. Google's guidelines say to provide a website that represents your individual business location ([Google, "Guidelines for representing your business on Google", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), accessed Oct. 2026). So even Google's own rules treat the website as a normal part of a business listing.

## What Does a Google Business Profile Do Well?

It puts your business in front of people who are already looking, and it gives them the basics fast. Name, category, hours, phone number, location or service area, photos and reviews all sit in one place, right where the search happens.

![Illustration of a map pin above a small business card with a star rating row and a phone icon](/images/blog/do-you-need-a-website-if-you-have-a-google-business-profile-1-profile.webp)

Three things make the profile worth the effort:

- **It is where local results live.** Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence, and that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking ([Google, "Improve your local ranking on Google", Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091), accessed Oct. 2026).
- **It gathers reviews in public.** Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking (same source).
- **It lets you interact with customers, once verified.** Google says that to edit info such as your business name or hours, and to interact with customers, you need to verify your business ([Google, "Verify your business", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242), accessed Oct. 2026).

That is a lot of value for a free listing. If you have not set yours up well yet, start with our [Google Business Profile optimization checklist](/en/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).

## What Can a Website Do That a Profile Cannot?

A website lets you tell the whole story in your own words, on your own terms. The profile gives you a fixed set of fields. A site gives you pages.

![Illustration of a laptop with a browser window showing content blocks, an image placeholder and a green button, next to a small stack of pages](/images/blog/do-you-need-a-website-if-you-have-a-google-business-profile-2-website.webp)

Here is where the difference shows up:

- **Room to explain.** Google's profile description is limited to 750 characters, cannot include URLs or HTML, and should focus on your business rather than promotions, prices or sales ([Google, "Edit your Business Profile", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617), accessed Oct. 2026). A website has no such cap. You can describe each service, who you serve and how you work.
- **Control over the rules.** The profile follows Google's guidelines on everything from your business name to your address and phone number. On your site, the layout, the wording and the pages are yours.
- **A page for each language.** If you serve customers in more than one language, you can write a page in each. Google says it uses the visible content of a page to determine its language, not code-level information such as `lang` attributes or the URL ([Google Search Central, "Managing multi-regional and multilingual sites"](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites), accessed Oct. 2026). So a page written in Spanish reads as Spanish. Our guide to a [bilingual website for a small business](/en/blog/bilingual-website-for-small-business) covers this in depth.
- **A home for ads.** If you run Google Ads, the page people land on is part of the picture. Google lists landing page experience as one of the three components of Quality Score, which it describes as a diagnostic tool and not an input in the ad auction ([Google, "About Quality Score for Search campaigns", Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118), accessed Oct. 2026). For when that page should be a full site and when a single landing page is enough, see [landing page vs website](/en/blog/landing-page-vs-website).

## Do the Profile and the Website Help Each Other?

Yes, and Google says so in a small but useful way. On its page about local ranking, Google explains that prominence "is also based on info like how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have" ([Google, "Improve your local ranking on Google", Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091), accessed Oct. 2026).

![Illustration of two puzzle pieces fitting together, one with a map pin and one with a browser window](/images/blog/do-you-need-a-website-if-you-have-a-google-business-profile-3-together.webp)

Read that carefully. Google does not say a website guarantees a better ranking, and it gives no formula. It says links from websites to your business are one of the signals behind prominence. A business with no website of its own has one fewer place to be linked from, and one fewer thing to link from.

So the pair works like this. The profile gets you found and gives people a fast way to call or visit. The website gives them somewhere to go next and gives other sites something to point to. Each makes the other more useful.

## What If You Only Have a Profile Right Now?

Then you are not doing anything wrong, and you do not need to panic. Many businesses start with a profile because it is free and quick. For a short stretch, it can carry your basic presence while you plan something better.

But treat it as a starting point, not a finish line. Three limits are worth knowing:

1. **Google owns the page.** The profile follows Google's guidelines, and you must verify your business to edit info such as your name or hours. The guidelines also say a business name must reflect your real-world name and that unnecessary information in it can lead to suspension ([Google, "Guidelines for representing your business on Google", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), accessed Oct. 2026). If something goes wrong with a profile, you have no other home to fall back on.
2. **The format is fixed.** Every business in your category looks similar in the results. A site is where you show what makes you different.
3. **It only reaches people on Google.** Someone who hears about you from a friend, sees your truck or reads a card may want a place to learn more. A web address is easy to share anywhere.

## How Should You Split the Job Between the Two?

Let the profile handle discovery and quick contact, and let the website handle explanation and trust. Use this table as a simple way to decide where something belongs.

![Illustration of a balance scale with a map pin on one side and a browser window on the other](/images/blog/do-you-need-a-website-if-you-have-a-google-business-profile-4-split.webp)

| What people need | Where it fits best | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours, phone, location or service area | Google Business Profile | It is right where the search happens |
| Reviews and ratings in public | Google Business Profile | Google collects them on the listing |
| A full list of services, in detail | Website | The profile description is limited and cannot include URLs |
| Pages in each language you serve | Website | You can write a full page per language |
| A page to send ad clicks to | Website or landing page | Landing page experience is part of Quality Score |
| Your story, your process, your answers | Website | You control the wording and the layout |

Notice that neither column is "better." The point is that each does the part it is built for. If you already have both and calls are still slow, the problem may be in how the site is set up. See [why a website is not getting calls](/en/blog/website-not-getting-calls) for a way to diagnose that.

## Does Your Website Need to Be Big or Expensive?

No. A clear, simple website that says what you do, where you work and how to reach you is a real asset. It does not need dozens of pages to be useful.

What matters is that it is accurate, easy to read on a phone and consistent with your profile. Your name, phone number and service area should match in both places. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a website if I have a Google Business Profile?

Yes, if you want a place that you fully control and that can explain your business in detail. The profile helps people find and contact you on Google, but it is a listing with fixed fields. A website covers what the profile cannot.

### Can a Google Business Profile replace a website?

No. They do different jobs. The profile is built for discovery and quick contact, and the website is built for explanation and trust. Google's own guidelines even tell you to provide a website that represents your business location.

### Does having a website help my Google Business Profile rank?

Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence, and that prominence also reflects how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have. It does not say a website guarantees a better ranking, and there is no way to pay for one.

### Is a Google Business Profile enough for a new business?

It can be a reasonable place to start, since it is free and quick to set up. But treat it as a first step. Google owns the listing and its rules, so a website gives you a home that you control.

### What should I put on my website that is not on my profile?

Detailed descriptions of each service, the areas you serve, pages in each language your customers speak and answers to common questions. The profile description is limited to 750 characters and cannot include URLs, so the site is the place for the rest.

Check that your profile and your website, if you have one, say the same thing. If you do not have a website yet, write down the five questions customers ask you most. Those questions are the outline of a simple first site.
