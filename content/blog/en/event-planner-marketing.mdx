Referrals built your business: a bride tells her sister, a venue manager passes your name along. That is a great way to book events, but you do not control it. When it slows down, you have nothing to turn up.

This guide adds a second way in: being found by a stranger who types "event planner near me" plus your town into Google.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Local search is mainly decided by relevance, distance and prominence. Google says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.
> - If you meet clients at their venues or at a coffee shop, you are a service-area business. Google tells you to remove your address from the profile in that case.
> - Your website is where a planner proves taste. Give your work, your services and your areas their own pages, and make the inquiry step easy.
> - Past clients are your best source of reviews. Ask every one the same way, with no gift or discount in return.

## Why Referrals Alone Leave Gaps

Word of mouth only reaches people already connected to someone you have worked for. A search reaches everyone else: the client who just moved to town, or the office manager handed an event nobody wants to organize. They do not have your name. They have a search box.

Even a referred client may look you up before writing. What they find then is part of the sale: real photos, a clear website and recent reviews reassure them. An empty result makes them wonder. None of this replaces referrals. It makes each one easier to close.

## How Google Decides Who Shows Up

![Illustration of a magnifying glass over a map with a green pin, beside a small stack of cards](/images/blog/event-planner-marketing-1-search.webp)

Google explains what drives local results. They are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance is how well your Business Profile matches what someone is searching for. Distance is how far each business is from the person searching. Prominence is how well-known a business is, and Google says it also depends on how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have ([Google, "Improve your local ranking on Google", Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091), accessed Oct. 2026).

You control relevance and part of prominence: how you describe your work, whether your website backs it up, and whether clients review you. You cannot pay to skip the line, because Google states there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

If your profile is not set up yet, start with our [Google Business Profile optimization checklist](/en/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist). This guide covers what is different when you plan events.

## Decide Your Address Situation First

![Illustration of a laptop on a home desk, a house icon and a map with overlapping circles and one green pin](/images/blog/event-planner-marketing-2-address.webp)

Most planners work from a home office or small studio and do the real work at venues and at tastings. That changes what you put on Google.

Google defines a service-area business as one that visits or delivers to customers directly but does not serve customers at its business address. It adds that if you do not serve customers at your business address, you should remove your address from your Business Profile ([Google, "Manage your service areas for service-area & hybrid businesses", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481), accessed Oct. 2026). The guidelines page also says a service-area business should hide its business address from customers.

Match that to the setups you are likely to have:

- **You work from home and meet clients at venues or by video.** You are a service-area business. Hide your home address and list the areas you serve.
- **You rent a studio or showroom where clients actually come to see samples.** Google calls a business that serves customers at its address but also visits or delivers to them a hybrid business. Read the guidelines page and answer honestly about how you operate.
- **You pay for a mailbox or a virtual office you never work from.** Google says that a rented mailing address where you do not operate, also known as a virtual office, is not eligible for a Business Profile, and that P.O. boxes or mailboxes at remote locations are not acceptable ([Google, "Guidelines for representing your business on Google", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), accessed Oct. 2026).

Two more rules from the same page: the phone number must be under the direct control of the business, and you should not create more than one page for each location. For the clicks, follow our guide to [setting up a service-area business on Google Business Profile](/en/blog/service-area-business-google-business-profile).

### Choose service areas that match where you plan events

Google says you can set up to 20 service areas based on the cities, postal codes or other areas you serve. It also says you cannot set your service area as a radius distance around your business.

Treat the limit as a ceiling: list the towns where you genuinely plan events. Distance still counts in local ranking, so a competitor closer to the searcher has an edge you cannot change in settings. You can change relevance and prominence. Keep categories tight too: Google says to use as few as possible to describe your core business, and that unnecessary information in a business name could result in suspension of your profile.

## Turn Your Website Into a Portfolio That Takes Inquiries

![Illustration of a laptop showing photo frames and a green envelope icon for an inquiry](/images/blog/event-planner-marketing-3-portfolio.webp)

In most businesses the website is a brochure. In yours, it is the audition: clients choose taste and trust, partly by looking at your work. The profile links to your site, and Google counts how many websites link to a business as part of prominence, so the two work as one system. Build the site to do three jobs.

**1. Show real work, organized the way clients think.** Give each type of event you handle its own page: weddings, corporate events, milestone birthdays, whatever you genuinely do. On each page, say who the event was for in general terms, what the client needed and what you handled. Use your own photos, with permission from the people involved. Do not present other vendors' photos as yours.

**2. Give each area you serve a page worth reading.** If you list a town on your profile, a visitor from that town should find something useful on your site: venues you work in, how you approach events there, what you handle. Write each one from scratch. Our guide to [service area pages for a local business website](/en/blog/service-area-pages-for-local-business-website) shows how to make each one useful.

**3. Make the next step obvious.** A visitor who loves your work should see how to reach you without hunting. Put a clear contact option on every page. Ask only for what you need to reply well: event type, date in mind, location and rough scale.

Keep name, phone number and areas consistent between profile and site. If your site needs work, see our [website design service](/en/services/website-design).

## Reviews From Past Clients Become Your Next Booking

![Illustration of a smartphone beside a row of five stars with one filled green and a folded card with a glass](/images/blog/event-planner-marketing-4-reviews.webp)

Clients remember the day and how calm you kept things, so the moment after the event is a natural time to ask.

Google says prominence also depends on how many reviews you have. In the BrightLocal 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults, 85% said they are more likely to use a business after reading positive reviews, and 80% said they are more likely to use a business that replies to all of its reviews ([BrightLocal, 2026, via PinMeTo](https://www.pinmeto.com/news/brightlocal-local-consumer-review-survey-2026/), accessed Oct. 2026). Those are survey answers about stated preferences, not proof of what any one person will do. PinMeTo sells local presence software, so we note that as context.

### Ask in a way Google allows

- **Use the link or QR code from your profile.** Google says businesses can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code to leave a review, and that customers need a Google Account to do it. The QR code can currently only be generated on a computer browser, not on a phone ([Google, "Create a Google link or QR code to request reviews", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815), accessed Oct. 2026).
- **Never offer a gift or discount for a review.** Google calls offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews "fake & misleading content" and says it is strictly prohibited ([Google, "Ask for Google reviews", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122), accessed Oct. 2026). For a planner, that includes a free add-on at the next event.
- **Ask every client, not only the happy ones.** Google's content policy lists as prohibited discouraging negative reviews, or selectively soliciting positive reviews from customers ([Google, "Prohibited & restricted content", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7400114), accessed Oct. 2026).
- **Do not pressure people on site.** Google's policy says merchants should not require or pressure users to leave ratings or write reviews while on the premises ([Google, Maps User Generated Content Policy Help](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114), accessed Oct. 2026). Send the request after the event.
- **Keep conflicts of interest out.** The policy bars content based on a conflict of interest, which may include current or former employment or a contractual relationship. Your florist or photographer should refer you, not review you.

### Reply to every review

Before you can reply to reviews, you must verify your business, and the reviewer is notified when you reply ([Google, "Manage customer reviews", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050), accessed Oct. 2026). Google recommends replies that are professional, polite, short and direct, not used to advertise deals. Add something specific to the event, and never share private details about a client or guests. For harder cases, see [how to respond to negative Google reviews](/en/blog/how-to-respond-to-negative-google-reviews), and for the asking habit, [how to get Google reviews for your local business](/en/blog/how-to-get-google-reviews-for-local-business).

## A Simple Order of Work

1. **Decide your address situation** and hide your home address if clients do not come to you.
2. **Verify the profile**, pick tight categories and list only areas you serve.
3. **Build portfolio pages** by event type, plus one useful page per area, with a clear way to inquire.
4. **Ask every past client** for a review, with your profile's link and no incentive.
5. **Reply to every review.**

The same order works in other trades: see [real estate agent marketing](/en/blog/real-estate-agent-marketing) for a close parallel, or [marketing for local service businesses, by industry](/en/blog/marketing-for-local-service-businesses-by-industry). Local search rewards a profile and site that stay accurate and active, and Google gives no schedule for when any of it pays off.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do event planners get clients online?

Mainly by being found when someone searches for a planner in their area. That means a verified Google Business Profile with the right address setup, a website with portfolio pages and a way to inquire, and reviews from past clients. Referrals still matter, and this adds a way for strangers to find you.

### Should an event planner show a home address on Google?

If you do not serve customers at your home address, Google says to remove your address from your Business Profile. A service-area business should hide its business address from customers, so most planners who work from home keep their address private.

### Can I offer a discount for a review?

No. Google says offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews is strictly prohibited. Ask every client in the same way, with your profile's review link or QR code, and do not offer anything in return.

### How many service areas can I list?

Google says you can set up to 20 service areas based on cities, postal codes or other areas you serve. List only the places where you really plan events.
