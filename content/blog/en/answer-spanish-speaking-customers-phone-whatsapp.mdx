A customer finds your business, taps the call button and starts talking. Halfway through the first sentence, you realize they are speaking Spanish, and you are the only one in the shop. What happens next may decide whether that person books a job with you or hangs up and tries the next listing.

Most advice about Spanish-speaking customers stops at the click: the ad, the keywords, the website. This guide starts after it, when a Spanish speaker calls, texts or writes on WhatsApp. It is written for the owner who is not fluent in Spanish and wants a practical setup, not a promise the business cannot keep.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Nationally, most people who speak another language at home speak Spanish, and most Spanish speakers also speak English "very well." Being able to use English is not the same as choosing it, so the language of your first reply still matters.
> - Decide who answers Spanish calls, texts and WhatsApp messages before you advertise in Spanish.
> - Answer in the language the customer used, and never advertise "we speak Spanish" unless a real person can handle the whole conversation.
> - Use automatic messages as a hold, not a replacement, and keep price, address and schedule details in the hands of someone who can confirm them in the customer's language.

## Why Does the Language of Your First Reply Matter?

Because the customer's first contact is also their first test of whether you can help. National data gives you a sense of the audience, though not of your town. In the Census Bureau's 2018 to 2022 American Community Survey, 78.3% of people age 5 and older spoke only English at home. Among those who spoke another language, 61.1% spoke Spanish ([U.S. Census Bureau, 2023](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023/language-at-home-acs-5-year.html), accessed Oct. 2026). By subtraction, that suggests roughly one in five people use another language at home. That last step is our inference, not a figure the Census published, and it is a national number. It tells you nothing about how many of your own customers speak Spanish.

The same release says 61.0% of Spanish speakers spoke English "very well." That does not mean they prefer English. Being able to use English and choosing to use it are different things. Someone who can fill out a form in English may still want to explain a leaking roof in Spanish.

Pew Research found a similar mix in media habits. In 2024, 54% of US Latinos got news mostly in English, 21% mostly in Spanish and 23% in both languages about equally ([Pew Research Center, 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/2024/03/19/english-and-spanish-language-news-consumption-among-hispanics/), accessed Oct. 2026). That is a news habit, not a buying habit, so treat it only as a hint that people move between languages.

One more data point comes with a warning label. A 2020 CSA Research survey across 29 countries found that "76% of online shoppers prefer to buy products with information in their native language" ([CSA Research, 2020](https://csa-research.com/l/media/Consumers-Prefer-their-Own-Language), accessed Oct. 2026). It is a global survey about online product information, not about phone calls in the US, so it only supports the general idea that language matters.

None of this says your customers prefer Spanish. It says you cannot assume they do not.

The customer repeats themselves, switches to English or moves on. We cannot tell you how often each happens, and we will not invent a number.

## How Do You Cover the Phone?

Name one person who answers Spanish calls, and give that person a backup. Then make sure everyone else knows what to do when a Spanish speaker reaches them first.

![Illustration of a desk phone, a smartphone with a green speech bubble above it and a notepad with a pen](/images/blog/answer-spanish-speaking-customers-phone-whatsapp-1-first-call.webp)

A workable setup has four parts:

1. **An owner for the language.** It may be you, a teammate, an office manager or a trusted partner who speaks Spanish. What matters is that someone is clearly responsible, and that the person is allowed to handle the first conversation from start to finish.
2. **A plan for the person who picks up and cannot continue.** The worst outcome is silence or a hurried "no Spanish." Teach everyone a short, kind handoff line (below).
3. **A voicemail greeting in both languages.** Record it once, with your business name and what to leave: name, number and what the customer needs.
4. **A callback habit.** A voicemail only helps if someone calls back, in the language the customer used.

If you are the only person in the business and you do not speak Spanish, be honest about the limit. Plan for the call that arrives anyway: a greeting, a way to take a message and a callback you can actually keep. Hiring or training someone is a business decision we cannot price for you, because we have no source for what it costs.

### What Can Anyone Say in Spanish When the Call Comes In?

Anyone on your team can learn two short lines. Ask a Spanish speaker you trust to check the wording for your region and your business before you use them, because phrasing varies between communities.

> "Hola, gracias por llamar a [business]. ¿En qué le puedo ayudar?"
>
> "Un momento, por favor. Voy a buscar a alguien que le pueda ayudar en español."

The first is a greeting. The second is a handoff that tells the caller help is coming. Using "usted" in a first contact is a respectful default; follow the customer's lead if they relax the tone.

## How Should You Handle Text Messages?

Reply in the language of the message, and keep it short. A customer who texts has picked a channel, and the reply should meet them there. Match their language and their channel.

![Illustration of a sheet of paper with a pen next to two speech bubbles, one graphite and one green](/images/blog/answer-spanish-speaking-customers-phone-whatsapp-2-script.webp)

Here is a simple structure you can adapt to your trade and your voice:

> "Hola [name], le habla [your name] de [business]. Gracias por escribirnos sobre [the problem]. ¿Me puede decir la dirección del trabajo? Con gusto le llamo cuando le convenga."

Three habits keep these replies useful:

- **Use the customer's own words.** If they wrote "gotera en el techo," say so back. It shows you read it.
- **Ask one question, not five.**
- **Do not promise a price.** A first reply is not a quote, in any language.

If you do not speak Spanish yourself, a translation tool can help you read an incoming message and draft a reply. We have no verified data on how accurate those tools are, so treat the output as a draft. Anything about price, address, schedule or scope should be checked by a person who reads Spanish before it goes out.

For the habit of replying first on every channel, see [speed to lead for a local service business](/en/blog/speed-to-lead-local-service-business).

## How Should You Use WhatsApp for Customers?

If you add WhatsApp as a contact option, treat it like a phone line: someone owns it, someone checks it and someone answers it. WhatsApp describes its Business app as one that "é destinado a um número de telefone" (it is meant for one phone number) ([WhatsApp, "A diferença entre o WhatsApp e o WhatsApp para Empresas"](https://whatsappbusiness.com/pt-br/resources/resource-library/whatsapp-vs-whatsapp-business/), accessed Oct. 2026). So decide up front whose phone holds that number and what happens when that person is on a job.

![Illustration of a smartphone with a chat and a green check mark, and a small clock face beside it](/images/blog/answer-spanish-speaking-customers-phone-whatsapp-3-whatsapp.webp)

The WhatsApp Business app also offers a business profile and automatic greeting and away messages, according to the company's own page for small businesses ([WhatsApp, "Por que pequenas empresas devem usar o app WhatsApp Business"](https://whatsappbusiness.com/pt-br/resources/resource-library/small-business-use-whatsapp-business-app/), accessed Oct. 2026). Both pages are in Portuguese, and we describe only what they say. They tell us nothing about how many of your customers use the app.

A good away message says who you are, what happens next and what details you need. Here is a version to adapt.

> "Hola, gracias por escribir a [business]. Recibimos su mensaje. Una persona le responderá en horario de atención. Si puede, cuéntenos qué necesita y en qué zona está."

An away message is a hold, not an answer. Make sure a real reply follows it, in the same language, and never let it promise a time you cannot keep.

One caution: do not open a channel you will not watch. An unanswered WhatsApp number looks worse than none.

## What Should Happen When You Hand the Customer Off?

The customer should never have to repeat their story. Whoever takes the first message writes down what they learned, and whoever continues the conversation reads it first.

![Illustration of two hands passing a clipboard with a green clip from one desk to another](/images/blog/answer-spanish-speaking-customers-phone-whatsapp-4-handoff.webp)

A handoff note needs only a few lines:

- The customer's name and the best way to reach them.
- The language they used.
- The problem, in their words, and the address or area.
- What was already promised, if anything.

That last line matters most. A business that says one thing on the first call and another on the second loses the customer's confidence, and that is hard to win back. The best place to keep these notes is the simple log from your [lead management routine](/en/blog/lead-management-for-small-business): date, name, language, channel and what happened next. Add a language column and you will see how many of your own contacts arrive in Spanish, a better number than any national figure.

## What Should You Not Promise?

Do not say "we speak Spanish" unless a person can handle the whole conversation: the call, the quote, the schedule and the follow-up. A line on your website or Google profile is a promise, and a broken promise is worse than silence.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a bilingual employee to serve Spanish-speaking customers?

Not necessarily, but someone must be able to handle the conversation if you advertise in Spanish. That can be a teammate, a partner or a person you train. We have no verified cost data, so judge the options against your own call and message log.

### Should I answer a Spanish text in Spanish?

Yes, reply in the language the customer used. If you do not speak Spanish, use a translation tool for a draft and have a Spanish speaker check price, address and schedule details first.

### Is WhatsApp the best channel for Spanish-speaking customers?

We cannot say. There is no verified data on how many Spanish-speaking customers prefer WhatsApp. Offer it only if you can check it, and keep the phone and text options open.

### Does an automatic reply count as answering?

It helps, but it is not a replacement. An automatic message confirms that the message arrived, and a person still needs to follow up with the details the customer asked for, in the same language.

### Should I say "we speak Spanish" on my website or profile?

Only if a person can handle the full conversation, from the first call to the follow-up. Otherwise, say what you can actually offer and fix the staffing first.
