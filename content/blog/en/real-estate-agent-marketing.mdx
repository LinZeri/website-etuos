Your past clients like you. Your Instagram followers like your posts. But when a stranger in your town types "real estate agent near me" into Google, does your name come up at all?

Being liked and being found are two different jobs. This guide covers the second one: how an agent gets found in local search, with the quirks that are specific to your business. Many agents work from a home office or a desk at a brokerage, and that changes how you handle your address on Google.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Local search is mainly decided by relevance, distance and prominence. Google says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.
> - If you do not meet clients at your own address, Google tells you to remove it from your profile. A virtual office or P.O. box does not qualify as a business address.
> - Reviews feed prominence, but Google bans incentives and selective asking. Ask every client the same way, and reply to what comes in.
> - Your website should back up the profile with a clear page for each area you actually serve.
> - Advertising rules for agents vary by state, so check yours and your brokerage's before you advertise.

## Why "Liked" Does Not Mean "Found"

Social posts, open house flyers and referrals all help people who already know you. A local search reaches people who do not. They are comparing options on a map, reading stars, and tapping the first few names that look real.

Google explains what decides who shows up. Local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance is how well your Business Profile matches what someone is searching for. Distance is how far each business is from the person searching. Prominence is how well-known a business is, and Google says it is also based on how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have ([Google, "Improve your local ranking on Google", Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091), accessed Oct. 2026).

Two things follow for you. First, you control relevance and part of prominence: your profile details, your reviews and the sites that mention you. Second, you cannot buy your way up the local results: Google states that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

## The Address Question Every Agent Hits First

![Illustration of a house key, a mailbox and a map pin, with a cluster of mailboxes crossed out in green](/images/blog/real-estate-agent-marketing-1-address.webp)

This is where agents most often go wrong, because the usual setups do not fit Google's definition of a business address.

Start with Google's guidelines page. If a business rents a physical mailing address but does not operate out of that location, also known as a virtual office, Google says that location is not eligible for a Business Profile. It also says P.O. boxes or mailboxes located at remote locations are not acceptable ([Google, "Guidelines for representing your business on Google", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), accessed Oct. 2026).

Now match that to the three common agent setups:

- **You work from home and meet clients at their homes, at showings or at a coffee shop.** You are a service-area business. Google says that if you do not serve customers at your business address, you should remove your address from your profile ([Google, "Manage your service areas for service-area & hybrid businesses", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481), accessed Oct. 2026). The same guidelines page says a service-area business should hide its business address from customers. Your home address stays private.
- **You rent a desk or a mailbox you never actually work from.** That is the virtual office or remote mailbox case above. Do not use it as your profile address.
- **You regularly work from a physical office and serve clients there.** Google defines a hybrid business as one that serves customers at its business address but also directly visits or delivers to them. If that describes you, read the guidelines page and answer honestly about where you really operate.

Whichever setup is yours, use a phone number that you control. Google's guidelines say the phone number must be under the direct control of the business. And keep the profile to one location: Google says not to create more than one page for each location of your business, in a single account or in several.

For the step-by-step on hiding an address and choosing areas, we already wrote it up in [how to set up a service-area business on Google Business Profile](/en/blog/service-area-business-google-business-profile). Follow that guide for the clicks, then come back here for the agent-specific choices.

## Pick Your Service Areas Like You Pick Your Listings

![Illustration of a street map with overlapping circles around neighborhoods and a single green pin](/images/blog/real-estate-agent-marketing-2-areas.webp)

Agents think in neighborhoods, school zones and towns. Your profile can reflect that. Google says you can set up to 20 service areas based on the cities, postal codes or other areas you serve.

Treat that limit as a ceiling, not a target. List the places where you really take clients.

The profile alone does not decide your position, because distance still counts. If a competitor sits closer to the person searching, that is a factor you cannot change from the settings page. What you can change is relevance and prominence, which is why the next sections matter.

A few habits help:

1. **Choose areas you know.** The places you can talk about with real detail are the places worth listing.
2. **Describe your work in plain words.** Say what you do for buyers and for sellers, and in which areas. Do not stuff the name field. Google says that unnecessary information in a business name is not permitted and could result in the suspension of your Business Profile.
3. **Keep categories tight.** Google says to use as few categories as possible to describe your overall core business from the list it provides.

## Reviews Are Your Prominence Engine, Within Google's Rules

![Illustration of a smartphone beside a row of five stars with one filled green, and a folded thank-you card](/images/blog/real-estate-agent-marketing-3-reviews.webp)

Choosing an agent is a big decision, so reviews do a lot of work. They are how a stranger decides whether you are real. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking.

The buyers' side agrees. In the BrightLocal 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults, 85% said they are more likely to use a business after reading positive reviews, and 80% said they are more likely to use a business that replies to all of its reviews ([BrightLocal, 2026, via PinMeTo](https://www.pinmeto.com/news/brightlocal-local-consumer-review-survey-2026/), accessed Oct. 2026). Those are survey answers about stated preferences, not proof of what any one person will do. PinMeTo sells local presence software, so we note that as context.

### How to ask without breaking a rule

Google's guidance is simple, and the rules matter because agents often ask at the emotional high point of a closing:

- **Use the link or QR code from your profile.** Google says customers can leave a review through a link or by scanning a QR code, and that they need a Google Account to do it. In your profile, you can open the reviews area and use the share option. Google notes that the QR code can currently only be generated on a computer browser, not on a phone ([Google, "Create a Google link or QR code to request reviews", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815), accessed Oct. 2026). Menu names can change, so check the current page.
- **Never trade something for a review.** Google calls offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews "fake & misleading content" and says it is strictly prohibited ([Google, "Ask for Google reviews", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122), accessed Oct. 2026). That includes gift cards, closing gifts and discounts, even when they feel like a thank-you.
- **Ask everyone, not only the happy ones.** Google's content policy lists as prohibited discouraging negative reviews, or selectively soliciting positive reviews from customers ([Google, "Prohibited & restricted content", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7400114), accessed Oct. 2026). Send the same request to every client.
- **Do not pressure people.** Google's policy says merchants should not require or pressure users to leave ratings or write reviews while on the premises ([Google, "Prohibited & restricted content", Maps User Generated Content Policy Help](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114), accessed Oct. 2026).
- **Keep conflicts of interest out.** The policy bars content that is based on a conflict of interest, which may include current or former employment. So a review from your own staff or a colleague is a bad idea.

A natural handoff point exists for agents: the thank-you note or the follow-up message after closing, where you can include the link with a short, honest request.

### How to reply

Before you can reply to reviews, you must verify your business, and the person who wrote the review is notified when you reply ([Google, "Manage customer reviews", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050), accessed Oct. 2026). Google asks for replies that are professional, polite, short and relevant, and not used to offer promotions. Never share a reviewer's private details in a reply. If an issue is complex, invite the person to continue by phone or email.

Because a deal involves private information about people's homes and finances, that last rule matters more for you than for most. ## Let Your Website Back Up the Profile

![Illustration of a laptop showing simple page blocks with a house icon and a green map pin](/images/blog/real-estate-agent-marketing-4-website.webp)

Google counts how many websites link to your business as part of prominence, and the profile links to your site, so your site is part of the picture. Think of your website as the place where you can say more than a profile allows.

Three practical moves:

1. **Give each area you serve its own page.** Write something a neighbor would actually find useful about that area and how you work there. Avoid copying one page and swapping the town name. Our guide to [service area pages for a local business website](/en/blog/service-area-pages-for-local-business-website) explains how to make each one worth reading.
2. **Make the profile and the site agree.** Your name, phone number and the areas you list should match on both. A mismatch looks sloppy to clients.
3. **Make the next step obvious.** A visitor should see how to call, message or book a conversation without hunting for it. If your site does not do that yet, take a look at our [website design service](/en/services/website-design).

## One Line on Advertising Rules

Advertising rules for real estate agents vary by state. Before you advertise, check the rules in your state and your brokerage's own advertising rules, because this guide is marketing advice and not legal guidance.

## A Simple Order of Work

If you are starting from zero, go in this order:

1. **Decide your address situation** using the three setups above, and remove your home address if you do not serve clients there.
2. **Verify the profile** and choose a tight set of categories.
3. **Pick the service areas** where you actually work.
4. **Add a review request** to the end of every client relationship, using your profile link, with no incentive and no filtering.
5. **Reply to every review** in a short, professional way.
6. **Build one useful page per area** on your website, and keep the details consistent with your profile.

Then keep going. Local search rewards a profile that stays accurate and active, and Google gives no schedule for when any of it pays off.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should a real estate agent use a home address on Google Business Profile?

If you do not serve customers at your home address, Google says to remove your address from your Business Profile. A service-area business should hide its business address from customers, so most agents who work from home keep their address private.

### Can I use a virtual office or P.O. box as my address?

No. Google says that a rented mailing address where you do not operate, also known as a virtual office, is not eligible for a Business Profile, and that P.O. boxes or mailboxes at remote locations are not acceptable.

### How many service areas can I list?

Google says you can set up to 20 service areas based on cities, postal codes or other areas you serve. List only the places where you really take clients.

### Can I give clients a gift for leaving a review?

No. Google says offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews is strictly prohibited. Ask every client in the same way, with your profile's review link or QR code, and do not offer anything in return.

### Can I pay to rank higher in local results?

No. Google states that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Relevance, distance and prominence decide local results, and reviews and links to your website feed prominence.
