The safest way to choose an SEO company is to ignore the promise and inspect the proof: what exactly it will do, what it can honestly claim, how it reports, and who owns what when the work is done. Anyone who guarantees a ranking is telling you something Google itself says nobody can sell.

One disclosure before we start: Etuos sells SEO, so we have an interest in this topic. This guide is written to help you judge any SEO provider, and that includes us. Apply every check below to us as well. For the broader checklist on hiring any agency, read [how to choose a marketing agency](/en/blog/how-to-choose-a-marketing-agency-local-business). This post is only about SEO promises and SEO reports.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Google says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking, and that it does not guarantee it will crawl, index or serve a page. A guaranteed position is not a promise a provider can keep.
> - A good proposal lists tasks you can verify. A weak one lists buzzwords, "strategy" and a package that looks the same for every business.
> - Ask for a sample report. Clicks, impressions, click-through rate and average position are defined by Google. Your own calls and quote requests matter more than any of them.
> - Pages that only swap a city name are a spam pattern in Google's policies. Ask what is unique on each page they plan to build.
> - Keep ownership simple: your accounts are in your name and the provider works inside them.

## What an SEO Company Can and Cannot Control

![Illustration of a magnifying glass over a map pin, a padlock and an open hand](/images/blog/how-to-choose-an-seo-company-local-business-1-limits.webp)

A provider can improve what you control: your site, your Business Profile, your content and the way you measure results. It cannot control what Google decides to show.

Google is direct about the local side. On its help page about local ranking it says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence, and that "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google" ([Google, "Improve your local ranking on Google", Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091), accessed Oct. 2026). Distance is the one you cannot change, because it is how far you are from the person searching.

Google also says that its Search works in stages, that not all pages make it through each stage, and that it does not guarantee it will crawl, index or serve your page ([Google Search Central, "In-depth guide to how Google Search works"](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/how-search-works), accessed Oct. 2026). Even the basic step of getting a page into Google has no promise attached. If you want to learn what good local SEO covers, our [complete guide to local SEO for service businesses](/en/blog/local-seo-for-service-businesses-complete-guide) explains it, so we will not repeat it here.

The filter is simple: a provider can describe the work it will do, but it cannot honestly sell you a position, a number of leads or a date.

## Check 1: They Do Not Guarantee a Ranking

Be most careful with the confident sentence: "We guarantee page one." Here is why it fails, in plain terms:

- Google says you cannot pay for a better local ranking, so no fee can buy a position.
- Google does not guarantee that it will crawl, index or serve your page, so even a published page has no promised place.
- Google says a sitemap does not guarantee that everything in it will be crawled and indexed ([Google Search Central, "Learn about sitemaps"](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/overview), accessed Oct. 2026). And in the URL Inspection tool, it says submitting a request does not guarantee that the page will appear in the Google Index ([Google, "URL Inspection tool", Search Console Help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9012289), accessed Oct. 2026).

Watch for softer versions of the same promise: "first page in a set time," "a guaranteed number of calls," "we never lose a ranking." They all claim control over something that is not theirs. A trustworthy provider describes inputs, such as the pages it will fix or write, the profile work and how results are measured, and is honest that outcomes depend on things outside its control. Treat any fixed deadline for results as a sales line.

## Check 2: The Proposal Lists Work You Can Verify

![Illustration of a clipboard checklist with a pencil next to a small shop front](/images/blog/how-to-choose-an-seo-company-local-business-2-work.webp)

A proposal should read like a work plan, not a brochure. Compare these two descriptions:

- **Weak:** "Comprehensive SEO strategy, advanced optimization, premium link building and authority growth."
- **Better:** "Fix the title and heading on these service pages, complete the Business Profile, set up measurement of calls and form submissions, write one page per service you actually offer, and report on the results."

The first cannot be checked. The second can. Ask: "What will you do first, what will you do next, and how will I see that it was done?"

Then check three areas where weak providers hide.

**The pages they plan to build.** If the plan includes many location pages, ask what makes each one different. Google's spam policies describe doorway abuse as sites or pages created to rank for specific, similar search queries, and they list pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page as an example ([Google Search Central, "Spam Policies for Google Web Search"](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), accessed Oct. 2026). That does not make every city page a problem, but a page that only changes the city name is a risk, not an asset. Google also suggests asking whether content provides original information, reporting, research or analysis, and whether it provides substantial value compared to other pages in search results ([Google Search Central, "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content"](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), accessed Oct. 2026). Ask the provider to show you a draft page and judge it with those two questions. If you want a deeper look at this topic, see our guide to [service area pages for a local business website](/en/blog/service-area-pages-for-local-business-website).

**The profile work.** Local visibility also runs through your Business Profile. Google says prominence depends partly on how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking ([Google, "Improve your local ranking on Google", Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091), accessed Oct. 2026). So ask how the provider plans to earn reviews and links honestly, and what they will do for your listing. Our walkthrough on [how to rank higher on Google Maps](/en/blog/how-to-rank-higher-on-google-maps) shows what that work looks like.

**The one-size package.** A proposal identical for a plumber, a dentist and a restaurant has not looked at your business. Ask what is specific to your services and area.

## Check 3: The Report Measures Your Business, Not Their Activity

![Illustration of a report page with plain blocks next to a phone handset and an envelope](/images/blog/how-to-choose-an-seo-company-local-business-3-reports.webp)

Ask to see a sample report before you sign. Then compare it with what Google actually measures. In the Search Console performance report, Google defines clicks as the number of times a user clicked your site from Google Search results, impressions as how many times your site appeared in Search results, and click-through rate as the click count divided by the impression count ([Google, "Performance report (Search results)", Search Console Help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553), accessed Oct. 2026).

Average position needs extra care. In the chart view, Google defines it as the average position of the topmost result from your site. In the table view, it is the average position for the specific URL or grouping shown in that row. A provider who quotes one "average position" without saying which view it comes from may be comparing two different things.

Here is what an honest report tends to include:

1. **Business results first.** Calls, form submissions and booked jobs, with a clear definition of what counts as a lead and how spam and wrong numbers are removed.
2. **Search Console numbers with their definitions.** Clicks, impressions, click-through rate and position, explained in words you can follow.
3. **Pages and indexing.** Google says "Not indexed is not necessarily bad" and that being indexed does not guarantee a page will show up in your search results ([Google, "Page indexing report", Search Console Help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7440203), accessed Oct. 2026). A report that celebrates "pages indexed" as a result is stretching it.
4. **What was done and what comes next.** A plain list of completed tasks and the next ones.

A report that is a wall of impressions and "reach," with no calls, measures effort, not your business. Google says you do not have to sign up for Search Console to be included in search results ([Google, "About Search Console", Search Console Help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9128668), accessed Oct. 2026), so it is a window for you, not a requirement for ranking. If you want to read it yourself, our guide to [Google Search Console for local business owners](/en/blog/google-search-console-for-local-business-owners) walks through it.

## Check 4: You Own the Accounts

This gets one short paragraph here because it has its own guide. Google says a verified owner has the highest degree of permissions in Search Console ([Google, "Verify your site ownership", Search Console Help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9008080), accessed Oct. 2026). Whoever holds that role holds the data about your own site. Your Search Console property, Business Profile and ad accounts should be in your name, with the provider added as a user, and you should know who controls your domain settings. For the questions to ask and a simple audit table, read [who owns your Google Ads account and Business Profile](/en/blog/who-owns-your-google-ads-and-business-profile-agency).

## Red Flags in One List

- A guaranteed ranking, a guaranteed number of leads or a guaranteed date.
- "Secret" methods the provider will not explain.
- No list of tasks, only a package name.
- Many near-identical city pages as the main plan.
- Reports with no calls, form fills or jobs.
- A provider that wants to own your accounts, or a contract that does not explain how to leave.

A contract is a legal document: have a lawyer review it before you sign. This guide gives you questions to ask, not legal advice.

## Questions to Ask an SEO Company Before You Sign

![Illustration of two speech bubbles facing each other over a table with a stack of papers](/images/blog/how-to-choose-an-seo-company-local-business-4-questions.webp)

Use these in the call and ask for the answers in writing.

1. What will you do first, and how will I see that it was done?
2. What can you not promise, and why?
3. Which pages will you create or change, and what makes each one different?
4. How will you earn reviews and links honestly?
5. What does your report include? Can I see a sample?
6. How do you count a lead, and how do you remove spam and wrong numbers?
7. Who owns my Search Console property, Business Profile and domain?
8. What happens to my site, content and data if we stop working together?
9. What have you advised a client not to do?

The last question is a test. A provider that has never talked a client out of something is probably only selling.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I choose an SEO company?

Ignore the guarantee and check the proof. Ask what exactly the company will do, what it will not promise, what its report includes and who will own your accounts. Compare two or three providers on the same questions, in writing.

### Can an SEO company guarantee a first-page ranking?

No. Google says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google, and that it does not guarantee it will crawl, index or serve your page. Treat a guaranteed position as a red flag.

### What should an SEO report include?

Your business results first, such as calls and form submissions, then Search Console numbers with their definitions: clicks, impressions, click-through rate and average position. It should also list the work done and the work planned next.

### What are red flags when hiring an SEO company?

Guaranteed rankings, secret methods, no list of tasks, near-identical city pages, reports without calls or inquiries, and a provider that wants to own your accounts.

### Do I need to own my Search Console and Business Profile?

Yes, you should. Google says a verified owner has the highest degree of permissions in Search Console. Have the provider work inside accounts that are in your name.
