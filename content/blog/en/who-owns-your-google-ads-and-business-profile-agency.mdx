You should own your Google Ads account, your Google Business Profile and your Search Console property, and the agency you hire should work inside them as a user. If an agency owns them instead, you are renting your own marketing, and you may lose the history, the listing and the ad data the day you leave.

One disclosure before we start: Etuos is a marketing agency, so we have an interest in this topic. Apply every check below to us as well. This guide is only about accounts, logins and ownership. > **Key Takeaways**
> - Three accounts carry your marketing: Google Ads, Google Business Profile and Search Console. Each one should be in your name, with the agency added as a user.
> - Google ties editing a Business Profile and interacting with customers to verification, and says a verified owner has the highest degree of permissions in Search Console. Control follows ownership.
> - Never accept a fix that involves creating a second profile for the same business. Google says not to.
> - Ask three plain questions: who is the owner of each account, do I have full administrator access, and who can see the billing.
> - This is practical advice, not legal advice. Have a lawyer review any contract before you sign.

## Why Ownership Matters More Than Any Promise

![Illustration of an open hand holding one large key with a green tag next to three smaller keys](/images/blog/who-owns-your-google-ads-and-business-profile-agency-1-owner.webp)

An agency's promises are words. Account ownership is a fact you can check. The person who owns an account decides who gets in, who gets removed and who can see what is inside. If that person is the agency, you depend on its goodwill for access to your own business listing, your own ad history and your own site data.

This applies to every agency, including a good one. A good agency has no reason to object, because it works fine as a user.

## Google Business Profile: You Should Be the Owner

![Illustration of a small storefront with a map pin above it and a green padlock on the door](/images/blog/who-owns-your-google-ads-and-business-profile-agency-2-profile.webp)

Your Business Profile is the listing people see on Google Search and Maps. It is the most visible thing an agency touches, and the easiest to lose control of.

Google says you need to verify your business to edit your business info, such as the name or hours, and to interact with customers ([Google, "Verify your business", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242), accessed Oct. 2026). In plain terms, verification is the gate. Whoever controls the verified profile controls what the public sees.

Here is what to ask:

- **Who is the primary owner of the profile?** The answer should be you, or someone at your company, using an email address your business controls.
- **Am I able to manage it directly?** You should be able to sign in and see the profile yourself, with full administrator access, not only receive screenshots.
- **Is the agency added as a user?** That is the normal setup: the agency does the work from its own login, and you decide whether it keeps access.

Be especially careful if the profile already exists. If an agency created it before you ever saw it, check that it is a single, correct listing for your business. Google's guidelines say not to create more than one page for each location of your business, in a single account or in multiple accounts ([Google, "Guidelines for representing your business on Google", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), accessed Oct. 2026). Duplicate listings made under different accounts make control messy.

The same rule applies when something goes wrong. If a profile is suspended or disabled, Google's help page says: "Do not create a new Business Profile for the same business" ([Google, "Fix suspended or disabled profiles", Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145), accessed Oct. 2026). An agency that proposes starting a fresh listing to get around an access problem is offering a shortcut Google tells you not to take. If you want to know what a healthy profile should contain, our [Google Business Profile optimization checklist](/en/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) goes through it. And if your listing is not appearing where you expect, see [why your business is not showing on Google Maps](/en/blog/why-my-business-not-showing-google-maps).

## Google Ads: The Account Should Be in Your Name

![Illustration of a blank ledger, a payment card with a green arrow and a megaphone](/images/blog/who-owns-your-google-ads-and-business-profile-agency-3-ads.webp)

Google Ads is where the money moves, so ownership matters most here. We will keep this part practical and avoid describing how Google structures its access levels, because that is a platform detail you should confirm inside your own account. The advice does not depend on it.

Ask these three questions before any campaign is built:

1. **Is the Google Ads account in my name?** It should belong to your business, created with your business information, not set up so that your campaigns live somewhere only the agency can open.
2. **Will I have administrator access?** You should be able to open the account, see the campaigns, the keywords, the ads and the spending, and add or remove other users.
3. **Who sees the billing?** Your ad money is your money. You should be able to see what is being charged, and to whom the payments go. If you pay the agency for ad spend instead of paying Google, ask in writing how that money is passed on and how you will see the proof.

Why does this matter beyond control? Your account holds your history: which keywords brought real customers, which ads worked, which searches wasted money. That history is an asset. If you leave and the account stays with the agency, the next team starts from zero and you pay to relearn what you already paid to learn. 

A related check: Google keeps a searchable repository of advertisers and the ads they have served, searchable by advertiser or website name and filterable by date and targeted location ([Google, "Ads transparency", Advertising Policies Help](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/13733850), accessed Oct. 2026). 

## Search Console and Your Website

![Illustration of a browser window with a house icon and a key with a green edge](/images/blog/who-owns-your-google-ads-and-business-profile-agency-4-site.webp)

Search Console is Google's free tool for understanding how Google sees your site. It is not required to appear in search results, but it is the best window you have into your own visibility, and the data inside it belongs with the business.

Google defines ownership verification as proving to Search Console that you own a specific website, and says a verified owner has the highest degree of permissions ([Google, "Verify your site ownership", Search Console Help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9008080), accessed Oct. 2026). That sentence is the whole argument. If the agency is the verified owner and you are not, the agency holds the highest permissions over data about your own website.

There is a second layer. For a Domain property, Google lists verification through your domain name provider's DNS settings as the only way ([Google, "Verify your site ownership", Search Console Help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9008080), accessed Oct. 2026). It follows that whoever controls your domain name settings also matters here. So ask two more questions:

- **Is the domain registered in my name, under an email I control?** If the agency registered it, find out and ask for it to be transferred to you.
- **Who can change the domain's DNS settings?** You should know, and you should be one of the people who can.

If you want to learn to read the data once you have access, our guide to [Google Search Console for local business owners](/en/blog/google-search-console-for-local-business-owners) walks through it. ## A Simple Access Audit You Can Run Today

Sit down with a notepad. For each account, write the answer to four questions:

| Account | Who is the owner? | Can I sign in myself? | Who else has access? | Who can remove users? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | | | | |
| Google Ads | | | | |
| Search Console | | | | |
| Domain name and DNS | | | | |

If you cannot fill in a row, that is your first task. If the owner of any row is a person you cannot name, or an agency you no longer work with, treat it as an open risk. Ask your current agency, in writing, to add you as owner or administrator, and keep a copy of the reply.

## What to Say to an Agency About Ownership

You do not need legal language. Plain sentences work, and the way an agency answers tells you a lot.

- "Before we start, I want the Business Profile, Google Ads account and Search Console in my business's name, with your team added as users."
- "Please confirm in writing who will be the owner of each one."
- "I want full administrator access before the work starts."
- "If we stop working together, what exactly happens to the accounts, the data and the website files?"

A last point on contracts: what a contract says about accounts, data and ending the relationship is a legal question, and the answers depend on the document in front of you. Have a lawyer review it. This article gives you questions to ask, not legal advice.

## What If an Agency Already Owns Your Accounts?

If you are already in this position, stay calm and work in order.

1. **List what exists.** Use the audit table above to find out which accounts exist and who owns each one.
2. **Ask for access in writing.** Request owner or administrator access, and name the accounts. Keep it polite and specific.
3. **Do not create a duplicate profile as a workaround.** Google's guidance is to avoid more than one page per location and to avoid a new profile for the same business. Duplicates make the original problem worse.
4. **Get help with the transfer.** If the agency will not cooperate, use Google's help pages for the specific account, or ask a professional who handles account recovery.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who should own my Google Ads account?

You should. The account should be in your business's name, with you holding administrator access and the agency added as a user. Ask who the owner is before any campaign is built.

### Who should own my Google Business Profile?

You, or someone at your business, using an email address your business controls. Google says you need to verify your business to edit your business info and interact with customers, so whoever controls the verified profile controls the listing.

### What if my agency created my Google Business Profile?

Ask to be added as an owner or administrator, and ask in writing. Check that only one profile exists for each location, because Google says not to create more than one page for each location.

### Why does Search Console ownership matter?

Google says a verified owner has the highest degree of permissions in Search Console. If the agency is the only verified owner, it holds the highest permissions over data about your own site.

### What should I ask an agency about account ownership?

Ask who will own each account, whether you will have full administrator access from the start, who can see the billing, and what happens to the accounts, data and website files if you leave.
