### Introduction (100-150 words)
- Hook: There are roughly 2.4 million janitor and building cleaner jobs in the United States (BLS, 2025, source N7). Competition is enormous, and most owners respond by cutting prices.
- Problem: Underpricing fills next week, but traps you in a cycle of chasing the next cheap job.
- Promise: This is a channel-by-channel playbook sequenced by budget and impact, covering GBP, reviews, ads, and the one variable nobody talks about (route density).
- Key Takeaways box placement (after hook, before first H2).

### H2: Why Recurring Clients Change the Math (250-300 words)
- **Answer-first**: A bi-weekly residential client at $200/visit generates roughly $10,000 over two years. Even a $200 acquisition cost is trivial against that lifetime value.
- Cover: residential vs. commercial buying cycles, recurring contracts as the real goal, why pricing decisions should be based on lifetime value instead of first-job revenue.
- **Chart**: Horizontal bar chart comparing first-job revenue vs. 2-year lifetime value for weekly, bi-weekly, and monthly cleaning clients (illustrative arithmetic, labeled as such).
- **Key stat**: Use illustrative arithmetic ($200/visit x 26 visits/year = $5,200/year x 2 = $10,400), explicitly labeled "illustrative."

### H2: Start With Google Business Profile (300-350 words)
- **Answer-first**: GBP signals account for 32% of local pack ranking (Whitespark, 2026, source S7). For cleaning companies under $500/month in marketing budget, optimizing GBP is the highest-ROI first move.
- Cover: primary category selection ("House Cleaning Service" vs. "Cleaning Service"), service area setup, photo uploads, Google Posts.
- **Key stat**: Listings with 100+ images receive 520% more calls than the average listing (BrightLocal GBP Insights, source S8).
- **Internal link**: [Google Business Profile optimization checklist](/en/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).

### H2: Reviews Are the Trust Layer (250-300 words)
- **Answer-first**: In 2026, 31% of consumers will only use a business rated 4.5 stars or higher, nearly double the prior year (BrightLocal, 2026, source S9).
- Cover: asking for reviews after the first recurring clean (not the first job), responding to every review, handling negative reviews.
- **Key stat**: S9 (31% threshold at 4.5 stars).
- **Internal link**: [local SEO guide](/en/blog/local-seo-for-service-businesses-complete-guide) for the full review management framework.

### H2: Route Density Beats Scattered Growth (250-300 words)
- **Answer-first**: Booking 10 clients in the same three-block area earns more per hour than 10 clients spread across 30 miles. Route density is the single most underrated growth variable for cleaning companies.
- Cover: why geography is the core lever (not just a convenience), how to target marketing by zip code, choosing which neighborhoods to advertise in, declining jobs outside the route.
- **Unique insight**: Two crews losing 45 minutes a day to avoidable driving waste 7.5 labor hours per five-day week before fuel is counted. Tightening routes recovers hours already on the payroll.
- No external stat needed: this section is operator logic and illustrative arithmetic.

### H2: When to Add Paid Ads (and Which Kind First) (250-300 words)
- **Answer-first**: Once GBP and reviews are solid, paid ads accelerate what is already working. The two options for cleaning companies are Local Services Ads (pay per lead, trust badge) and Google Search Ads (pay per click, more control).
- Cover: LSA vs. Google Search Ads for cleaning, budget sequencing (GBP first, then LSA, then Search), why social media ads are a secondary channel for cleaning (low intent).
- **Internal link**: [how much paid ads cost](/en/blog/how-much-paid-ads-cost-local-business-guide) for cost benchmarks across trades, and [paid ads service page](/en/services/paid-ads).
- No fabricated CPC/CPL numbers. Refer reader to the paid ads cost guide for industry comparisons.

### H2: The Bilingual Advantage (200-250 words)
- **Answer-first**: In markets with Spanish- and Portuguese-speaking populations, a cleaning company with bilingual crews and bilingual marketing reaches customers that English-only competitors never see.
- Cover: bilingual Google Business Profile descriptions, running ads in Spanish at lower competition, answering the phone in the customer's language.
- **Internal link**: [selling to multilingual customers](/en/blog/selling-to-english-spanish-portuguese-speaking-customers) for the full multilingual playbook.
- **Key stat**: None required (the bilingual angle is the insight itself; the multilingual post carries the supporting data).

### H2: Residential vs. Commercial: Two Different Playbooks (200-250 words)
- **Answer-first**: Residential cleaning is a trust-and-convenience sale (the client lets you into their home). Commercial cleaning is a bid-and-documentation sale (insurance, contracts, property manager approval). Marketing each requires a different channel and timeline.
- Cover: commercial takes longer but pays more per contract, residential relies on reviews and referrals, commercial relies on outreach and proposals.
- No external stat needed: this is the structural insight that differentiates the post from generic listicles.
