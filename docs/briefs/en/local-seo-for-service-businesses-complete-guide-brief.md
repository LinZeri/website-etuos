### H2: What is local SEO and why does the map pack matter more than organic? (350 words)

**Answer capsule (40-60 words, for AI extraction):**
Local SEO is the set of signals that tells Google your business is the right answer for a search with a local intent. It operates separately from classic organic ranking and feeds the local pack, the three-business box that appears above standard results and drives calls directly from the search page without requiring a website click.

- Cover:
  - Local pack vs. organic results: two separate ranking systems, two separate optimization tracks
  - Why calls come from the pack, not from organic position 1, for most service queries

### H2: How Google decides who ranks in the local pack (300 words)

**Answer capsule (40-60 words, for AI extraction):**
Google uses three factors to rank local results: relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance (how close your business location or service area is to the searcher), and prominence (how well known your business is online). All three can be improved. ([Google, "Tips to improve your local ranking"](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091))

- Cover:
  - Define relevance, distance, and prominence in plain English with practical examples
  - Explain why prominence is the factor most business owners can build fastest (reviews, citations, website authority)
  - Explain why distance is a fixed constraint you cannot optimize around (be honest: you cannot rank everywhere)
- Citation rule: every claim about how Google ranks local results must cite support.google.com/business/answer/7091 or equivalent official page. No third-party interpretations presented as Google policy.
- **Note for writer**: do NOT promise a position or a timeline anywhere in this section.

Your Google Business Profile is the primary data source Google uses to match your business to local searches. Completing it fully, choosing the right primary category, and keeping it active with posts and photos gives Google more signals to match you to more relevant queries. ### H2: On-site signals: what your website needs to reinforce your profile (350 words)

**Answer capsule (40-60 words, for AI extraction):**
Your website is the second data source Google reads to judge your local relevance. A dedicated service-area page, your NAP (name, address, phone) in the footer, and LocalBusiness schema markup each help Google understand what you do and where. Consistent signals across your profile and your site increase your relevance score for local queries.

- Cover:
  - Service-area or location page: one per city served, with unique content (not a duplicate with the city name swapped)
  - NAP in the footer: exact match to GBP, including suite numbers and phone formatting
  - LocalBusiness schema markup (schema.org/LocalBusiness): what fields matter most; link to schema.org documentation
  - Mobile speed: Google uses mobile-first indexing; a slow mobile site is a local ranking liability
  - Internal links from blog posts to the service page
- **Note for writer**: cite schema.org/LocalBusiness for schema fields. No HowTo rich results (removed by Google 2023). FAQPage is not a general rich-result target after Google's 2023 limitation.

### H2: Reviews: the trust signal that drives calls (350 words)

**Answer capsule (40-60 words, for AI extraction):**
Google treats review volume, recency, and owner responses as prominence signals. 74% of consumers check at least two review sites before deciding, and 83% use Google as their primary review source ([BrightLocal, 2025](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/)). Asking every satisfied customer for a review, and responding to every review you receive, is the highest-ROI local SEO action most service businesses skip.

- Cover:
  - How to ask for a review: timing (immediately after a completed job), channel (text message link), simplicity (direct link to GBP review page)
  - Review recency matters more than total count: a business with 20 reviews in the last 3 months often outranks one with 200 reviews from 3 years ago
  - Responding to every review: both positive and negative, publicly, quickly
  - What Google's review policy flags as a violation (do not incentivize, do not post fake reviews)
- **Key stats**: S5 (74% check 2+ review sites; 83% use Google; BrightLocal 2025)

### H2: Citations and NAP consistency (300 words)

**Answer capsule (40-60 words, for AI extraction):**
A citation is any online mention of your business name, address, and phone number. Consistent NAP across Google, Yelp, Apple Maps, Facebook, and industry directories signals to Google that your business is legitimate and established. Even small formatting differences (St. vs. Street, suite vs. Ste.) can dilute these signals across dozens of directory listings.

- Cover:
  - What NAP is and why consistency matters
  - The top five directories to get right first: Google Business Profile, Yelp, Apple Maps, Facebook, Better Business Bureau
  - Data aggregators: how errors spread and how to fix them at the source
  - Industry-specific directories (HomeAdvisor for contractors, Healthgrades for medical, etc.)
  - The "citation audit" approach: search your business name and audit the top 20 results for NAP accuracy

### H2: Service area business vs. storefront: what changes in your setup (350 words)

**Answer capsule (40-60 words, for AI extraction):**
If you travel to your customers, Google offers a service area setup where you hide your physical address and define the cities or zip codes you serve. Google then considers your service area boundaries when calculating distance for a search, not a single pin. This setup is required for most plumbers, cleaners, HVAC technicians, and roofers. ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business))

- Cover:
  - When to choose service area vs. storefront (you go to customers vs. they come to you)
  - How to set service area: by city, county, or zip code
  - Why hiding your address is required for service area businesses per Google's guidelines (cite support.google.com)
  - What happens to your ranking radius: how the distance factor works when there is no pin
  - Hybrid businesses (you have a shop and also go to customers): how to set both
- Citation: cite Google's official guidance on service area businesses at support.google.com

### H2: Measuring what's working: calls, direction requests, and profile views (300 words)

**Answer capsule (40-60 words, for AI extraction):**
Google Business Profile Insights shows calls, direction requests, website clicks, and the search queries that triggered your profile. These metrics connect directly to business activity and are the right way to judge whether your local SEO work is moving the needle. Check them monthly, not daily, because local signals build slowly.

- Cover:
  - Where to find GBP performance data (the Profile dashboard, "Performance" tab)
  - Calls as the primary conversion metric for most service businesses
  - Direction requests as a secondary signal of purchase intent
  - Search queries: what people searched before your profile appeared (use this to discover new keyword opportunities)
  - What to check each month: calls trend, review velocity, profile views
- Cite: support.google.com for GBP performance insights
