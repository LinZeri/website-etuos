### H2-5: Multilingual Paid Ads Without Doubling the Budget (300-350 words)

**Answer capsule (50 words):**
A bilingual Google Ads campaign uses one shared budget split across separate ad groups by language. English and Spanish audiences peak at different times, search with different query patterns, and respond to different calls to action. One campaign structure, two audiences - the total reach increases while the budget stays the same.

**Cover:**
- Campaign architecture: one campaign with language-segmented ad groups vs. separate campaigns (when each approach makes sense)
- Shared budget logic: daily spend balances automatically across ad groups based on search volume at that time
- Ad scheduling by language: service searches spike at different times for different communities
- When to separate into distinct campaigns (higher spend, different service areas, different phone numbers)

**[ORIGINAL DATA]:** From Etuos campaign management: a bilingual structure with shared budget often results in lower overall cost per lead than doubling down on English alone, because Spanish ad groups fill daytime hours when English competition is highest. Frame as: "In the campaigns we manage for service businesses..."

**[PERSONAL EXPERIENCE]:** Practical checklist before launching bilingual paid ads:
1. Separate keyword lists for each language (do not auto-translate)
2. Language-specific landing page or at minimum a language-specific call-to-action
3. Call assets set up in the language of the ad
4. Someone on the team who can take a call in that language

### Optional FAQ (4 items, 200-250 words total)

1. **Do I need a separate website for Spanish-speaking customers?**
   No - a separate website is not required. A translated service page or two, a bilingual GBP description, and Spanish ad groups pointing to a language-aware landing page are enough to start. A full separate site is only justified at high spend volume.

2. **How do I know if my city has enough Spanish speakers to make bilingual marketing worth it?**
   Start with your own inquiry data: how many of your calls or messages come in Spanish already? If even 10-15% of your inbound contacts are in Spanish, a bilingual campaign will likely increase that. The cost of testing is low; the cost of ignoring it is a permanent share of your market going to a competitor who showed up in that language.

3. **Should I hire a Spanish-speaking team member before running Spanish ads?**
   Yes, ideally. Running Spanish ads to an English-only phone team wastes the click. At minimum, have one person who can take an initial call and schedule in Spanish. The ad can even reference this: "Hablamos espanol."

4. **Can I just translate my English ads into Spanish?**
   Translation is a starting point, not a strategy. Spanish queries are different from English queries. A translated ad with an English-based keyword list will miss most of the actual Spanish search volume. Start with native Spanish keyword research, not a translation of your English list.
